# Rain audio: source texture, spatial scale and surface response

Research review, 26 October 2026. Recommendation and experiments, not an implemented replacement or a claim of perceptual validation.

All 18 numerical cases agree to float64 precision; listening acceptance and spatial integration remain pending. This avoids a lossy statistical replacement at the first stage while still decoupling mixing cost from per-drop filter state.

**That audition was also rejected as sounding bad.** Fine-cache and fresh rain outputs differ by 1.95% relative RMS, but numerical similarity is not a listening pass. **Recorded-drop search and trial:** the user requested the old comparison again and online sources whose drops can accumulate into rain. The [candidate search and audition](benchmarks/rain-drops-2026-10-26/README.md) identifies two purpose-built paid libraries and uses CC0 concrete/metal drip previews for a small overlapping-snippet test. Three trigger densities are volume matched for texture comparison. These are empirical impact snippets with recording acoustics retained, not calibrated dry droplets. **The user rejected this audition as “horrible”.** Stop iterating on these snippets; neither bank is approved for integration. The PMSFX library's published demo page was opened for source review; its files have not been purchased or evaluated locally.

**Publisher source review:** the user authorized checking the library demos. Four PMSFX samples were found in the official Sonniss GDC 2020 track list and prepared as a [private source audition](benchmarks/rain-library-2026-10-26/README.md). They contain finished rain and designed layers, not the full library's isolated-drop bank. No new synthesis or propagation is applied, and source acceptance remains pending.

The best candidate for this project is a hybrid: model rainfall as many varied surface impacts, synthesize the dense component in bounded world-space regions, and preserve distinguishable impacts where spatial detail matters. Use the existing geometry/transport for shelter and propagation. Evaluate this against a small explicit-drop reference before choosing the runtime representation. A bank of convincing sounds solves a different problem from a stable spatial estimator; both need attention.

## Why the current model fails even at the right average energy

Our [weather audit](benchmarks/weather-review-2026-10-26/README.md) already establishes the separate transport-seed problem. The source has another limitation: `GroundDrop.wav` is one synthetic 24 ms transient repeated about 128 times/s across 4,988 m². A fully contained 5 m horizontal-radius disk sees only about two candidate impacts/s before shelter. The amplitude compensation represents many missing impacts with a few strong ones. Those are numerical samples of an area-power model, not a physically calibrated drop population.

An illustrative derivation makes the distinction precise. For independent Poisson event times, amplitude marks `a`, and a fixed kernel `h`, let `x(t) = Σ aᵢ h(t−tᵢ)`. Its second and fourth cumulants are:

```text
κ₂ = λ E[a²] ∫ h(t)² dt
κ₄ = λ E[a⁴] ∫ h(t)⁴ dt
```

Keep only fraction `p` of events and multiply surviving amplitudes by `1/√p`. The variance stays unchanged, but `κ₄` and the excess kurtosis increase by `1/p`. At `p = 0.01`, this idealized process is 100 times more impulsive by that measure despite equal average power. This is our mathematical illustration, not a measurement of Church: Church currently uses jittered cadence rather than a Poisson process. It explains why RMS-preserving source thinning cannot establish texture equivalence.

Likewise, ray contributions are numerical integration samples, not additional physical raindrop arrivals. A large inverse-probability transport contribution must not be interpreted as a genuinely louder drop when validating event statistics. The seed sensitivity must be repaired or converged before those rendered statistics can serve as a reference.

## Minimal controls and separate responsibilities

| Owner | Proposed controls | What remains internal or derived |
|---|---|---|
| Rain activity | Intensity, seed, shared wind | Event timing/size distribution, using a documented distribution preset; no per-drop inspector controls. Start with relative intensity until source power is calibrated. |
| Surface | One impact-response asset/preset; wet-state input when supported | Response variation and spectral/decay details live in the asset. Geometry and shelter determine which surface receives excitation. |
| Acoustic material | Existing propagation properties | Absorption/transmission act after emission. They do not manufacture a measured impact sound. |
| Rendering quality | Spatial approximation and ray/filter budgets | Quality changes may change approximation error, but must not redefine rainfall intensity, seed or dry source realization. |
| Future vibrating object | Existing density, elasticity and loss, plus geometry/support | Structural response and radiation remain deferred. Material identity alone does not specify a roof assembly. |

If we later expose intensity in mm/h, use a size distribution and fall velocity consistently. Atmospheric number concentration `n(D)` becomes surface flux through `q(D) = n(D) max(0, −v_rel(D)·normal)`, using velocity relative to the surface. On an unobstructed horizontal patch, integrating drop volume times flux must recover the declared rain rate with consistent units. Treat this as a conservation check, not a source-power calibration. A drop-size histogram in a volume is not directly an impacts-per-second distribution.

Marshall–Palmer is a compact empirical starting point, not a universal law. Measurements and analyses show variability among rain regimes and departures at the small/large-size ends; the distribution should be a versioned preset rather than an unexplained constant. See the [original 1948 paper](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/atsc/5/4/1520-0469_1948_005_0165_tdorws_2_0_co_2.xml) and [Testud et al. 2000, §drop-size distribution](https://journals.ametsoc.org/abstract/journals/atot/17/3/1520-0426_2000_017_0332_trpaat_2_0_co_2.xml). The flux equation above is our dimensional conversion, not a new empirical fit.

## Recommended experiment and architecture implications

**First compare three source representations on one small surface.** Keep the experiment offline and cheap: the existing weighted sparse ticks as a failure baseline; an explicit dense impact mixture as a reference for the chosen source model; and a compact statistical mixture of the same response family. Include a real recording for listening, with its surface, microphone position and recording acoustics identified. A synthetic reference verifies an approximation to the chosen model; it does not prove the model matches real rain.

**Use two response families initially:** a non-resonant hard/wet surface and water. Obtain or author several plausible dry variations, with independent noise where appropriate. Validate water's audible result against an airborne reference. Do not add a bubble tone to every solid hit, and do not treat arbitrary snippets from a reverberant rain recording as isolated dry drops. A response bank can represent empirical surface character without activating the deferred modal solver; it must be labeled empirical. Check correlation between regional streams: different offsets into one looping recording do not guarantee independent excitation or remove audible repetition.

**Then compare spatial approximations.** First-hit projection is still useful for deciding where rain lands. Start with fixed world-space patches so there is no adaptive scheduling/LOD machinery to debug. Group only where surface response and propagation are sufficiently similar; compare against explicit positions near the listener, across roof edges, and around openings. A patch waveform at one point has a common propagation path for all its constituent drops, so its spatial error must be measured. There is no researched universal patch size to copy into this scene.

**Keep source identity separate from the listener.** The dry field has a source ID, version, seed and absolute sample clock. Canonical world cells/time blocks can provide reproducible streams; the renderer samples their transfer. A dense statistical field need not instantiate every unresolvable physical drop. Events that are individually represented keep their own world identity and must be excluded from the aggregate contribution. Parent/child patch changes must not create a new independent rain realization or double its power. Defer adaptive grouping until fixed patches show a useful quality/cost tradeoff.

**Do not allocate one ordinary source pipeline per drop or mesh.** Existing `Emission.Batch` and `RayTransport.Layout` pack per-emission transport histories, which is expensive for dense rain. The matched single-listener rain run used approximately 1.36 GiB of numeric working storage. A compact source bank alone will not remove that cost. The candidate needs bounded excitation buffers and transfer histories per independently propagated region. Sharing one region's path response among nearby independent impacts is an approximation to validate, not a change that follows automatically from a seed.

**Preserve the current propagation responsibilities.** Keep 32 bounces, causal tails and the shared listener/material processing. The ELD method is a strong conceptual and diagnostic reference, but its precomputed aggregate playback is not a replacement for exact transient replay, moving doors or coherent bells. In particular, do not synthesize an extra far-rain soundtrack after the room renderer and thereby bypass shelter. A statistical late component would need an explicit, non-overlapping partition of paths and validation of its own.

**Indoor roof sound has two routes.** Airborne rain reaches the room through openings/transmission; impact-driven roof vibration radiates separately. We can improve the first route under the current scope. Realistic drumming requires either a measured/authored roof-radiation approximation or the later structural model. Neither follows from changing `CopperRoof` from stone to a guessed metal preset. Do not combine two representations of the same mechanism and count its power twice.

This is a proposed integration strategy, not a direct port of any paper. The source-generation contract and independent PCM/state per region still require design in this repository; present emission phase offsets into one shared clip are not arbitrary independent generators.

## What to measure before promoting a candidate

1. **Dry-source listening:** rain likeness, repetitive character, excessive ticks/tones and surface identity. Compare level-matched auditions with the matching gain recorded; keep original outputs. Listen to more than one seed and intensity. A spectrogram alone cannot accept a source.
2. **Texture:** short-window level distribution, crest factor/kurtosis, envelope modulation, cross-band envelope correlation, and the rate/strength distribution of distinguishable events. These supplement listening; none is a universal realism score. Use the McDermott toolbox as a reference for definitions, not an assumed production dependency.
3. **Shelter and motion:** outside, near an exposed surface, under an open canopy, closed room, doorway crossing and rain stopping. Preserve opening direction, filtering and delayed tails at unchanged playback gain. Sparse near events should retain localization; dense rain should remain spatially extended.
4. **Independence and cost:** source hashes across batch sizes/listener counts, no duplicate energy at representation transitions, several transport seeds, and spatial/ray refinement. Report generation, ray work, filter/history storage and total runtime separately. One grain-bank memory number is not an end-to-end benchmark.

Choose the simplest candidate that passes the dry-source check, then the small shelter scene. Only after that repeat the Church walkthrough. Repair the known transport spikes using frozen source inputs in parallel with this conceptual source investigation, without masking them through gain, limiting or a favorable seed.
